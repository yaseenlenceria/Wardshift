Where enquiries leak

The Cost of an Undesigned Inbox.

Most practices don't lose enquiries dramatically — they lose them quietly, at the same four points, every week.

Unacknowledged forms

An enquiry arrives and sits silently. Without an immediate confirmation, the enquirer has no idea it was received — and keeps looking.

Slow responses

Days pass before anyone replies. By then, the prospective patient has often already booked elsewhere.

Unclear next steps

The practice responds, but nobody says what happens next. Momentum dies in the gap between reply and booking.

No follow-up record

Nothing is tracked, so nothing can be measured — and the same leak quietly repeats every week.

Every leak is measurable — and fixable.

The designed journey

Six Stages. No Ambiguity.

Every enquiry follows the same visible path. At each stage, someone — or something — owns what happens next.

  1. 01

    New Enquiry

    An enquiry arrives by form, phone note, or message — captured once, with its source.

    Acts: System

    Every enquiry is recorded with source, time and detail — nothing lives only in an inbox.

  2. 02

    Acknowledged

    The enquirer receives an immediate confirmation that sets expectations.

    Acts: System

    Automated administrative acknowledgement within minutes — never clinical content.

  3. 03

    Practice Contact

    The enquiry is routed to the right person on the administrative team.

    Acts: Practice

    Clear ownership and response-time expectations, matched to how the practice runs.

  4. 04

    Appointment Request

    The enquirer is offered clear appointment options with no friction.

    Acts: Practice

    Straightforward options and next steps — no ambiguity about what happens next.

  5. 05

    Booked

    The appointment is confirmed with preparation details.

    Acts: Practice

    Written confirmation plus the practical details a patient needs before attending.

  6. 06

    Administrative Follow-Up

    Reminders, instructions and records are handled automatically.
